# Orðrétt in English and Chinese, for native readers

The page's own words come in three languages: Icelandic (`#is`), British
English (`#en-gb`) and Taiwanese Mandarin in Traditional Chinese
(`#zh-tw`). The English and the Chinese were written with an AI assistant's
help, like the Icelandic (see `IS-STRINGS.md`), and want a native reader:
someone who reads British English for `en-gb`, and someone in Taiwan for
`zh-tw`. Corrections go in `page.html`, in the `T` table under
`/* ---------- languages`, then `bash ordrett/publish.sh`.

The record page, `saga.html`, comes in the same three languages, chosen
the same way and remembered with the front page's choice. Audrey's
English is the original; the Icelandic and the Chinese were translated
from it with an AI assistant's help. Each language is one `<article>` in
`saga.html`; corrections go there.

## What is never translated

- What the tables wrote. Every summary, every „viljum“ sentence, every
  highlighted note on the AI sheet stays in the Icelandic it was written
  in, by people or by machines, in every language.
- Places and topics. They are the tables' and Samtal's words.
- Samtal's question, „Hvernig samfélag viljum við skapa saman?“. It keeps
  its own words; in English and Chinese its meaning is given beneath.
- Samtal's attribution line, „Gögn: Samtal, félag til almannaheilla
  (talasaman.is), CC BY 4.0“, in the form Samtal asks for.
- The highlighters' stems. They mark Icelandic words, so they are
  Icelandic; only each highlighter's name is translated.

All of these are marked `lang="is"`, so a screen reader reading English or
Chinese says them as Icelandic. A gate checks that nothing with an
Icelandic letter, or one of the page's Icelandic words, is left unmarked
in English or Chinese.

## How the language is chosen

1. The address: `#is`, `#en-gb` or `#zh-tw`. It is remembered.
2. Else the language chosen before.
3. Else the browser's: Icelandic if it lists Icelandic anywhere (many
   people in Iceland keep their browser in English); otherwise the first
   of English or Chinese it lists, any variety; otherwise English.

The switcher at the top names each language in itself: Íslenska, English,
華文.

## Conventions

- **en-GB.** British spelling (colour, centred, levelled); ‘single’
  quotation marks; dates as „10 October 2026 at 21:49“.
- **zh-TW.** Taiwanese usage (人工智慧, 錄音, 摘要, 討論桌); full-width
  punctuation and 「」 quotation marks; a space between Chinese and Latin
  letters or numerals, as in the medley (以 Samtal 開放資料); dates as
  „2026 年 10 月 10 日 21:49“; 唐鳳 for Audrey Tang. Chinese is set in the
  reader's own Traditional Chinese sans (PingFang TC, Noto Sans TC,
  Microsoft JhengHei), on the page and on posters; nothing is downloaded
  for it.
- The page says, in each language, that AI helped write that language's
  version.
